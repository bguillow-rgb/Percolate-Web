What Is Pink Bourbon Coffee? Flavor, Origin & Worth It?

By Percolate · Published September 26, 2026

Quick Answer

Pink Bourbon is a rare Arabica coffee variety grown primarily in Colombia's Huila region whose cherries ripen to a distinctive salmon-pink color. It's prized for an intensely floral, fruit-forward flavor, jasmine, tropical fruit, honey, and despite its name, genetic research suggests it descends from Ethiopian landrace populations, not the Bourbon lineage.

Pink Bourbon has become one of those coffees you start seeing everywhere once you know what to look for: on the bags of top specialty roasters, in barista competition routines, and increasingly on cafe menus that list varietals the way a wine list lists grapes. If you’ve picked up a bag and wondered what the name actually means, whether the flavor lives up to the price, and why DNA research turned the whole origin story upside down, this guide covers all of it.

”I keep seeing ‘Pink Bourbon’ on bags — what even is it?”

A question we hear often:

Pink Bourbon is a rare Arabica coffee variety whose cherries ripen to a distinctive salmon-pink color rather than the deep red most people associate with ripe coffee fruit. That cherry color is where everything starts. It’s how producers first noticed this plant was different, and it’s where the name comes from.

Also known as Rose Bourbon, it has become one of the most sought-after coffees in the specialty world. The striking color, unusual flavor, and murky origin have sparked genuine curiosity among baristas, cuppers, and home brewers alike.

It grows mainly in southwestern Colombia, especially in Huila’s San Adolfo region and parts of Cauca. The beans have a salmon-pink hue when ripe, which sets them apart from typical red or yellow varieties at a glance.

What makes it more than a novelty is the cup. Pink Bourbon shows up repeatedly in tasting competitions because of its floral, fruit-forward complexity and bright acidity. More on what that actually tastes like in your mug below.

”Wait — is it actually related to Bourbon?”

Here’s where things get genuinely interesting, and the story matters if you’re choosing beans carefully.

For years, the working assumption in specialty coffee was that Pink Bourbon was either a natural mutation of Bourbon or a hybrid of Red and Yellow Bourbon. The pink cherry color seemed to support the hypothesis, and Colombian producers had been cataloguing it under that name since at least the 1980s. Producer Rodrigo of Aromas del Sur stated that his team discovered Pink Bourbon in 2013, believing it first appeared in the San Adolfo area of Acevedo, Huila, in the 1980s and originated from southern Huila.

Then the DNA results came in. Two rounds of testing settled the question: leaf samples tested by DNA Analytica in 2017, and a five-sample panel run in 2023 by the coffee-genetics lab RD2 Vision for importer Cafe Imports, both placed Pink Bourbon material with Ethiopian landrace populations. That puts it in the same family as Chiroso, Wush Wush, Ombligon, and Bourbon Aji, not the Bourbon lineage the name implies.

Pink Bourbon is not a Bourbon mutation. That’s a significant finding that reshapes how the specialty industry understands the variety. It also helps explain the cup quality: those are all Ethiopian-lineage varieties known for floral complexity and high scores, and Pink Bourbon belongs in that company alongside Gesha and Wush Wush for the same reason.

One important caveat: that does not prove every tree sold under the name is genetically identical. Coffee names often travel through visual selection and farm practice before DNA authentication catches up. For a buyer, “Pink Bourbon” reliably signals how the roaster and producer identify the lot. It does not guarantee a specific lineage or flavor profile without supporting traceability. Look for bags that name a specific farm, municipality, or region (Huila, Narino, Tolima, Cauca) as your first signal of accountability.

”What does Pink Bourbon actually taste like in the cup?”

This one comes up a lot:

Sweet, floral, and citrusy, with notes of honey, white flowers, and tropical fruit. That’s the short version. The longer version depends heavily on processing method and roast level, which we’ll break down below.

Floral aromas, jasmine, hibiscus, and rose petals most commonly, are a hallmark of washed Pink Bourbon and are reminiscent of high-end Ethiopian coffees. Tropical fruit notes like mango, passionfruit, lychee, and papaya bring a juicy sweetness. The body is usually silky and medium-light, with a finish that lingers with honeyed sweetness rather than bitterness or astringency.

If you’ve tasted a high-quality Ethiopian Yirgacheffe or a washed Gesha, you’ll recognize the flavor family immediately: bright, layered, and intensely aromatic. The distinction is that Pink Bourbon is Colombian-grown, which tends to give it a rounder body and slightly more approachable acidity than some Ethiopian lots.

Pink Bourbon shines most with light-to-medium roast profiles, where the floral aromatics, tropical fruit, silky body, and bright acidity all show clearly. Take it darker and those defining characteristics disappear. Past the second crack, flavors become dominantly smoky and bitter, and everything that makes Pink Bourbon worth seeking out is gone.

Pink Bourbon occupies a sweet spot: the flavor complexity of a competition-grade Ethiopian-lineage variety, with enough Colombian-origin structure to be approachable for home brewing. Gesha and Pink Bourbon lots dominate competition podiums not because judges favor novelty but because those varietals have higher flavor ceilings under the right conditions.

Pink Bourbon placed multiple lots among the top-ranked coffees at Cup of Excellence Colombia 2022. It has also been selected by baristas for World Barista Championship 2023 routines, and specifically featured in the 2023 WBC finals, where it helped Boram Um secure the championship title.

”How is Pink Bourbon grown — and why is it so hard to find?”

Readers frequently ask:

Pink Bourbon is resilient, relatively high-yielding, and shows strong resistance to coffee leaf rust. It adapts well to its environment. That said, it faces challenges from other diseases and fungi, and it requires careful cultivation. It also needs to be isolated from other varieties to maintain purity and ensure successful cross-pollination.

Most exceptional Pink Bourbon lots come from farms above 1,600 MASL. Altitude is key: slower cherry development at elevation concentrates sugars and allows the variety’s floral complexity to fully develop. High-altitude growing conditions across Huila and Cauca are a consistent thread in the best lots.

The scarcity is real, not marketing. The variety requires isolation from neighboring plots to stay genetically consistent, which limits how many farms can viably cultivate it. That constraint, combined with genuine consumer demand following the 2023 WBC, makes traceable lots genuinely hard to source. Two finalists used Pink Bourbon in their 2023 WBC routines, which thrust it into the international spotlight. With that attention came an inevitable problem: not everyone selling “Pink Bourbon coffee” is offering the real thing.

”What should I look for when buying Pink Bourbon?”

Expect to pay $20-$30 per 12-ounce bag. Anything significantly below that is a warning sign. Beyond price, look for traceability: a specific farm, municipality, or growing region such as Huila, Narino, Tolima, or Cauca on the bag.

Here’s a practical checklist for evaluating a Pink Bourbon purchase:

  • Named producer or farm: “Colombia Pink Bourbon” without a farm name is a red flag
  • Altitude listed: look for 1,600+ MASL
  • Processing method noted: washed lots offer the clearest floral expression; natural lots amplify fruit
  • Roast level: light to medium only; any darker and you’re paying for a name that no longer delivers
  • Roast date: within the past 4 weeks is ideal; this is a variety where freshness matters enormously

”How should I brew Pink Bourbon at home?”

A question we hear often:

The best approach is whole beans, ground fresh before use, brewed with a pour-over method. The reasoning is straightforward: paper-filtered pour-over removes the oils and sediment that can muddy delicate floral notes, letting the jasmine and tropical fruit aromatics carry clearly.

Practically, aim for:

  • Grind: medium-fine for V60 or Kalita Wave; medium for Chemex
  • Water temp: 94-96 degrees C (slightly higher than for fragile light roasts, since washed Colombian lots can handle it)
  • Ratio: 1:15 to 1:16 (e.g., 18 g coffee to 270-290 g water)
  • Bloom: 30-40 seconds with twice the coffee weight in water; Pink Bourbon’s CO2 off-gassing is notable when fresh

Avoid cold brew for a first encounter. The long extraction masks exactly the floral top notes that define the variety. Save cold brew for everyday beans; brew Pink Bourbon hot and pay attention.

Frequently asked questions

Is Pink Bourbon actually related to Bourbon coffee? Despite the name, genetic testing by RD2 Vision and DNA Analytica places Pink Bourbon’s lineage with Ethiopian landrace populations, the same family as Chiroso and Wush Wush, not the Bourbon cultivar. The name stuck because of the cherry color and its Colombian context, not confirmed genetics.

What does Pink Bourbon coffee taste like? Pink Bourbon is known for floral notes like jasmine and honeysuckle, tropical fruit (mango, lychee, passionfruit), honey-like sweetness, and bright but elegant acidity. The body is typically silky and medium-light, especially in washed lots. Natural-processed Pink Bourbon amplifies the fruit notes further.

Where is Pink Bourbon coffee grown? Pink Bourbon grows primarily in southwestern Colombia, especially Huila’s San Adolfo area of Acevedo, and in parts of Cauca and Narino. It thrives at high altitudes where temperature swings slow cherry development and concentrate sugars.

How much does Pink Bourbon coffee cost? Expect to pay roughly $20-$35 per 12-ounce bag for traceable Pink Bourbon from reputable specialty roasters. Bags priced significantly below this range are unlikely to be authentic, verified Pink Bourbon material.

What’s the best brew method for Pink Bourbon coffee? Pour-over methods (V60, Chemex, Kalita Wave) are the best choice because the clean paper filter preserves Pink Bourbon’s delicate floral and fruit character. Brew light to medium roast Pink Bourbon at around 94-96 degrees C with a 1:15-1:16 ratio. Avoid dark roasts, which erase the variety’s defining character.

Does processing method change how Pink Bourbon tastes? Yes, significantly. Washed Pink Bourbon delivers the clearest floral expression: jasmine and white flower aromatics are most prominent. Natural-processed lots amplify the tropical fruit character (mango, passionfruit) and add body. Honey-processed examples sit between the two. Your brewing style and personal taste preference should guide which processing you seek out.
