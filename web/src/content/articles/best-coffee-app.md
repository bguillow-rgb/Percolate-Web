Most people go looking for a coffee app after the same small failure. You brewed a great cup of the Ethiopian last Tuesday and can't remember the grind setting. Or the new bag tastes flat and you don't know if it's the beans, the water, or you. Or you bought a coffee you already knew you didn't like, because you forgot you'd had it before.

Those are three different problems, and the apps that fix them are mostly different apps. That's why "best coffee app" lists tend to be useless: they rank a brew timer against a bean tracker as if they did the same thing.

We build one of the apps below, Percolate. Read that section knowing it. We've tried to be plain about where it's the wrong pick.

Every rating, version and update date here comes from the US App Store listing, checked on 16 September 2026. Those numbers move, so check the listing before you decide.

## What should a coffee app actually do?

There are four jobs, and very few apps do more than two of them well.

1. **Walk you through a brew.** Dose, water, pour schedule, timer.
2. **Log what you did.** The grind and ratio, the water temperature and time, then how the cup tasted.
3. **Track the bags you own.** What's open, what's resting, what's nearly gone.
4. **Find your next bag.** Based on what you actually liked, not what's trending.

The first two are crowded and mature. Job three has filled in over the last two years. Job four gets the least attention. A few apps show roaster deals, but few try to learn what you like first, and that gap is the reason Percolate exists.

## Filtru: the most-rated of the bunch

Filtru is the most-rated specialty brewing app we could find on the US App Store, with 4.77 stars from 3,780 ratings. Version 4.7.3 came out on 10 September 2026. It's been on the store since 2017, which counts for something in a category where apps come and go.

Filtru tells you how much coffee to use and when and how much to pour, with a timer running alongside. There's a coffee log and espresso shot recording in there too, plus an extraction calculator. The odd one is the augmented-reality preview, which drops a kettle or dripper onto your counter before you buy it.

**Use it for:** learning a method and repeating a recipe you liked.

**Don't expect it to:** track the bags on your shelf or suggest what to buy next.

## Timer.Coffee: free, all of it

Timer.Coffee calls itself an open-source brewing timer and calculator. Its listing says every feature is free and donations are optional. There are 27 brewing methods in it, from the V60 and AeroPress to the Hario Switch. Grind settings save per recipe, and the timer shows on the Lock Screen through Live Activities. It also logs brews with tasting notes and tracks your bean inventory with AI label recognition.

The listing shows 4.99 stars from 133 ratings, and version 3.8.5 went out on 4 September 2026.

**Use it for:** a timer, a log and a bean list, with nothing to pay.

**Don't expect it to:** recommend coffees you haven't bought yet.

## Beanconqueror: the deepest log you can carry

Beanconqueror is the most detailed logging app in this comparison, and its listing says it's open source and free to use. The preparation list is long (V60 and espresso are both on it) and you can strip each method down to only the parameters you care about. Bean tracking is built in. You can scan or import roaster details, and the app keeps a running total of how much of each bag is left.

It's been on the store since 2018. The listing shows 4.91 stars from 139 ratings, and version 8.7.1 went out on 16 July 2026.

**Use it for:** espresso dial-in and serious filter logging.

**Don't expect it to:** be quick. The depth is the point, and depth means fields to fill in.

## Coffee Book: a journal that keeps your data to itself

Coffee Book is a brew journal built around repeating a good cup. Each brew can hold more than 20 variables, beans and grinder and water among them. There's an espresso dial-in log, and a Lock Screen widget starts the brew timer. Its listing says it doesn't collect any data, which some people will care about more than any feature.

The listing shows 4.67 stars from 83 ratings, and version 4.1.3 went out on 20 August 2026.

**Use it for:** a private, thorough log.

**Don't expect it to:** recommend anything.

## BeanBook: journal, tracker and bag scanner in one

BeanBook (on the store as Coffee Journal - BeanBook) came out in June 2025 and covers more ground than most of the apps here. The journal takes photos and tasting notes. The bean tracker follows roast dates and how long a bag has been resting. Point the camera at a bag and its AI scan fills in the roaster, origin and process for you, tasting notes included. There are recipes, and it also shows deals from specialty roasters.

The listing shows 4.85 stars from 27 ratings, and version 1.9.4 went out on 13 September 2026.

**Use it for:** one app that logs brews and tracks bags.

## Tasting Grounds and Coffi: the social one and the scoring one

**Tasting Grounds** (4.85 stars from 47 ratings, last updated 1 June 2026) is built around sharing. You log and rate your brews and can see what friends are drinking. It also shows how other people brewed and scored the same beans you have.

**Coffi** (4.79 stars, 19 ratings, updated 14 August 2026) centers on an AI bag scanner and guided tasting reviews. Its listing says those reviews are based on professional frameworks like the SCA's, which makes them a gentle way to learn structured tasting.

## How to choose, in two questions

**What's frustrating you right now?**

- My cups are inconsistent → a brew log: Beanconqueror or Coffee Book.
- I don't know how to brew with this dripper → a guide: Filtru or Timer.Coffee.
- I've lost track of what I own and how fresh it is → a bag tracker: BeanBook, Coffi or Timer.Coffee.
- I keep buying bags I don't love → a taste profile: Percolate.

**How much will you type?** Be honest about this one. The most thorough log is worthless if you stop filling it in by week three. If you won't log six variables before your first sip, pick an app that asks for fewer. If you're rotating a lot of bags, a [plain coffee journal](/articles/how-to-keep-a-coffee-journal) covers the basics before you commit to any app.

## What about espresso?

Espresso is where a log pays off fastest, because small changes in grind and dose move the shot a lot. Beanconqueror, Coffee Book and Filtru all log shots. Whatever you use, log the roast date: a bag three days off roast and the same bag three weeks later won't extract the same way at the same grind. Our [espresso dial-in guide](/articles/how-to-dial-in-espresso) covers the adjustments themselves.

## What we left out, and why

Café ordering apps from Starbucks, Dunkin' and Peet's dominate App Store search for "coffee," but they're loyalty programs, not tools for brewing at home. Companion apps for connected scales and grinders only matter if you own the device, and several of them carry low ratings on the store this month. And we left out single-purpose timers that haven't been updated since 2022 or earlier.
