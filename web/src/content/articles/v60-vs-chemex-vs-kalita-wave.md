Pour-over coffee is one of the clearest ways to taste what's actually in a bag of beans. No pressure, no automation, just hot water, gravity, and your grind. But once you decide to brew by hand, you face a genuinely consequential fork: the Hario V60, the Chemex, or the Kalita Wave.

These three are among the most recommended pour-over drippers in 2026, and while each produces good coffee, they do so through fundamentally different mechanisms, each suiting a different kind of brewer. The choice matters more than most people expect. Getting it wrong doesn't mean bad coffee forever, but it does mean fighting your equipment from day one.

## What's actually different between these three drippers?

All three work on the same basic principle: hot water poured over ground coffee in a filter, with gravity doing the rest. The differences come down to three design variables. The Chemex uses thick bonded paper filters that remove more oils and fine particles, producing the cleanest cup. The V60 uses thinner filters with a single large drain hole for faster flow and more oils in the cup. The Kalita Wave uses wavy filters with a flat bottom and three small drain holes for a moderate flow rate and balanced extraction.

Those design differences cascade into everything: how long water contacts the grounds, how forgiving the brewer is to small technique errors, and what your cup ultimately tastes like.

## How does each dripper taste different?

*A question we hear often:* home brewers who've only ever used one pour-over want to know what they're actually missing by not trying the others.

**Hario V60:** The V60 produces bright, clean cups that highlight origin characteristics. You'll taste more pronounced acidity and distinct flavor notes than you'd get from a Kalita Wave. The thinner filter preserves coffee oils that thicker paper would absorb, adding a subtle richness. Lighter roasts particularly shine here. The high extraction potential brings out floral notes, fruit flavors, and complex acidity that can get buried with other methods.

**Chemex:** The Chemex brews a cup that's crisp and tea-like, emphasizing sweetness and clarity while muting heavier body or bitterness. The thick filter and wide brewing area highlight bright, floral, and delicate flavor notes, but produce less body than either the V60 or Kalita Wave. Medium to dark roasts often do well in the Chemex, where natural bitterness gets moderated. Light roasts can taste bright and pleasant, though the filter strips some of the more fragile floral and fruit notes.

**Kalita Wave:** The Wave's paper filters sit between V60 and Chemex in thickness, which lands you a balanced cup with moderate body and clarity. The Wave is known for smooth coffee with less pronounced acidity than the V60. It's forgiving by design, a solid entry point for beginners or anyone who wants consistency without much fuss. It offers slightly less clarity than the V60, but compensates with more even extractions.

## Which dripper is hardest to use well?

*This one comes up a lot:* brewers who struggled with the V60 want to know if they're doing something wrong, or if the dripper itself is the culprit.

Usually it's both. The single large hole at the bottom of the V60 offers zero flow restriction, meaning your pour technique alone controls brew time. That makes the V60 incredibly versatile and genuinely demanding in equal measure. Inconsistent grind size creates uneven flow, channeling, and bitterness that no amount of careful pouring can fix.

The Kalita Wave is the opposite. Its flat bottom creates an even bed of coffee grounds, and the three small drainage holes restrict flow naturally. Your pour technique has much less impact on the final result than it does with the V60. The accordion-pleated wave filters also create an air gap between filter and brewer wall, letting air escape freely during brewing, which prevents channeling and keeps extraction stable.

The Chemex lands in the middle on forgiveness, but adds its own wrinkle: it actually performs better at larger batch sizes. Brewing small single-cup doses often results in under-extraction because the water-to-filter-surface ratio is off and flow moves too fast.

Skill demand, ranked from easiest to hardest: **Kalita Wave → Chemex → V60.**

## Does it really matter which filter I use?

*Readers frequently ask:* can I just swap filters between brewers to save money?

No, and this isn't a brand upsell. Each dripper requires its own specific filter because filter paper thickness, shape, and porosity are engineered to work with that dripper's specific geometry and drain rate. Putting a V60 filter in a Chemex changes the flow dynamics entirely. A Chemex filter in a V60 won't even seat correctly.

The Chemex uses thick bonded paper filters, about 20 to 30 percent thicker than V60 filters. That thickness absorbs more oils and fine particles, which is precisely what gives the Chemex its signature ultra-clean cup. Swap in a thinner filter and you lose the defining characteristic of the whole brewer. The Kalita Wave's accordion-pleated filters serve a structural purpose too: they create an air gap between filter and brewer wall that promotes even extraction across the flat bed.

One notable exception worth knowing: the ORIGAMI dripper (a newer competitor gaining traction in 2026) accepts both V60 cone filters and Kalita Wave flat-bottom filters, making it genuinely versatile. Use V60 filters for a faster, brighter brew, or Kalita filters for something more forgiving.

## What about batch size -- does that affect which one I should buy?

This is one of the most practical and underrated parts of the decision. If you brew solo most mornings, the V60 and Kalita Wave are natural fits, both optimized for single-serve doses in the 15-20g coffee range. The Chemex comes into its own when you're brewing for guests. It brews 3 to 4 cups at once and looks good on the table while doing it. The 6-cup and 8-cup Chemex models are genuinely designed for batch brewing, not single cups.

## What grind size do I use for each one?

Grind size is arguably the biggest lever you have with all three brewers. The design of each dripper affects flow rate, extraction time, and flavor clarity, so the right grind for one is wrong for another.

As a working guide: use **medium-fine** (think table salt) for the V60; **medium to medium-coarse** (coarse sand) for the Chemex; and **medium** for the Kalita Wave. Going too fine with the Chemex will slow drawdown past five minutes and risk bitterness. Going too coarse with the V60 produces a thin, under-extracted cup in under two minutes.

## So which one should I actually buy?

The honest answer depends on three things: your skill level, your usual batch size, and what you want to taste.

If you want consistent coffee without learning technique, the Kalita Wave is the pick. If you want to develop real barista skills, the V60 is the right challenge. If you care about design and want the cleanest possible cup, the Chemex delivers.

Most specialty coffee drinkers who buy multiple bags and brew at home will get the most return from the **V60**. It's the cheapest to buy and to run (V60 filters cost a fraction of Chemex filters), and it's the most responsive to the coffee itself, which means it actually rewards using better beans. The Kalita Wave is the better choice if you want reliable weekday coffee without ritual. The Chemex is worth it if you regularly brew for more than one person and treat the brewer as part of the experience.

## FAQs

**Which pour-over dripper is best for beginners?**
The Kalita Wave is the best starting point. Its flat-bottom design and three small drain holes slow and even out water flow, so minor pour inconsistencies don't ruin the cup. Most people make good coffee within their first three or four brews.

**Does the V60 or Chemex produce a cleaner cup?**
The Chemex produces a cleaner, more tea-like cup because its bonded paper filters are roughly 20-30% thicker than standard V60 filters, absorbing more oils and fine particles. The V60 lets more oils through, which adds a subtle richness and slightly more body.

**Can I use the same grind size for all three drippers?**
No. Each dripper has a different flow rate, so grind size needs to match. The V60 uses medium-fine; the Kalita Wave uses medium; the Chemex uses medium to medium-coarse. Using the wrong grind causes under- or over-extraction regardless of pour technique.

**Do I need a gooseneck kettle for all three?**
A gooseneck is highly recommended for all three, but the Kalita Wave is the most tolerant of a less precise pour. The V60 in particular amplifies every inconsistency in pour speed and direction, making a gooseneck essentially non-negotiable for good results.

**Which dripper is best for light roast single-origin coffees?**
The V60 is widely preferred for light roasts. Its thinner filter and fast flow rate preserve floral notes, fruit acidity, and the delicate origin characteristics that make single-origin specialty coffee interesting. The Chemex's thick filter can mute some of those more fragile notes.
